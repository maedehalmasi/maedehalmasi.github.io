01 / the through-line

It doesn't feel like a change
of direction to me.

From the outside, my path might look like a series of different fields: web development, financial systems, systems analysis, and now machine learning. To me, they are connected by the same impulse: wanting to understand what's happening underneath.

In software, I learned to ask where assumptions break, why services disagree, and how to make complicated behavior understandable to other people. A good diagram could clear up a week of confusion. A careful test could reveal a missing question.

These days, I'm asking similar questions about learning systems. What do they carry from one task to another? What makes a representation useful? How can confidence be earned rather than simply displayed?

The subject is changing. The habit of asking why isn't.
SIDE NOTE / 01

I am more interested in questions that survive a second look than answers that sound impressive on the first.

02 / things I've built

A few things the real world taught me.

This isn't my employment history. It's two parts of engineering work that have stayed with me. The dates, job titles, and longer lists belong in a CV.

[01]
MAKING COMPLEXITY LEGIBLE

Before a system can work, people need a shared picture of it.

Working with enterprise banking systems meant translating ambiguous needs into processes, data flows, interface specifications, and technical documentation. I also helped build a knowledge base spanning more than 30 products. The lesson I kept: clarity isn't decoration. It's infrastructure.

[02]
BUILDING FOR REAL CONDITIONS

The interesting questions often arrive after deployment.

I worked on backend services for large financial platforms, including API development, debugging, data validation, and production investigations. Those experiences made me value observable behavior over confident assumptions—and made reliable intelligent systems an especially compelling direction to explore.

03 / open questions

Questions that keep a tab open
in my head.

These are directions for reading, learning, and future experiments. I don't have finished answers for them—and that's part of the point. Select a question to expand it.

Q.01What survives when the task changes?+

A model can become very good at one task. But what has it learned that can travel elsewhere? I keep returning to representation learning because the possibility of reusable structure feels fundamental.

representation learning / transfer
Q.02Can a useful system admit doubt?+

Engineering made me cautious about outputs that look certain but cannot be inspected. I'm interested in how learning systems express uncertainty, especially when their answers influence real decisions.

reliable ML / uncertainty
Q.03How do words and images find common ground?+

Language and vision offer different clues about the world. I'm curious about what multimodal models connect, what they miss, and how we can tell genuine generalization from shortcuts.

multimodal learning / language + vision

NOTE 001 / SYSTEMS

A diagram is not the territory.

A diagram can make a system understandable. Then production reminds you of everything the diagram left out. I've learned to value both the abstraction and the evidence that challenges it.

NOTE 003 / QUESTIONS

Maybe precision is a kind of curiosity.

The questions that interest me most usually get smaller before they get bigger. What exactly failed? Which assumption is doing the work? What evidence would change my mind? The habit travels surprisingly well—from debugging to reading a paper.

05 / off-screen

There is more to a person

When I'm not thinking about systems, I'm often thinking in different languages, listening to a podcast that sends me down an unexpected rabbit hole, or finding room to think outdoors.
